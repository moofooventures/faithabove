## A Reluctant Prophet's Résumé

When God calls Jeremiah in chapter 1, the young man's first response isn't excitement -- it's panic. "Ah, Lord GOD! behold, I cannot speak: for I am a child" (v.6). Jeremiah was likely a teenager, maybe barely out of boyhood, being told he'd confront kings, priests, and an entire nation careening toward judgment. Before he can list his qualifications (he has none), God interrupts with verse 5, and it's worth noticing the order of the verbs: formed, knew, sanctified, ordained -- all before Jeremiah had done a single thing. His calling wasn't a reward for performance. It preceded his birth entirely.

## Purpose Isn't Discovered, It's Revealed

We often talk about purpose like it's buried treasure we have to go dig up through personality tests and trial and error. There's nothing wrong with self-reflection, but Jeremiah's story suggests something different: purpose is less something you find and more something you're told, often by a God who already had it settled before you had any say in the matter. The Hebrew word for "knew" here (yada) is the same intimate word used elsewhere for deep relational knowledge -- this isn't God glancing at a cosmic résumé. It's God knowing Jeremiah the way you know someone you've loved for years, except Jeremiah didn't exist yet.

This should unsettle the idea that you need to have your life sorted out before God can use you. Jeremiah's calling to the nations came with zero track record, zero confidence, and an immediate objection. God's answer to "I am a child" wasn't to argue Jeremiah into believing in himself -- it was "say not, I am a child: for thou shalt go to all that I shall send thee... for I am with thee" (v.7-8). The weight of the calling was never meant to rest on Jeremiah's shoulders alone.

## Living Like You Were Known First

Most of us won't be called to confront nations, but the pattern holds: your sense of calling doesn't have to wait until you feel ready, qualified, or old enough. If you've been putting off a sense of purpose because you don't have the credentials yet, consider that Jeremiah's qualifications were supplied after the call, not before it -- God promises to "put my words in thy mouth" (v.9), touching Jeremiah's lips in the same scene. The equipping follows the calling.

This also reframes identity. If God knew you and set you apart before you were born, your purpose isn't something you have to manufacture from nothing or prove to anyone. It's something to walk into, one obedient step at a time, the same way Jeremiah did -- imperfectly, with real fear, for over forty years of a genuinely hard ministry. He wasn't given an easy calling; he was given a certain one. That's often closer to what purpose actually feels like: not a thrill of certainty about outcomes, but a steady conviction that you were placed here, now, on purpose, by Someone who knew you before you took your first breath.

PRAYER: God, thank You for knowing me before I ever had a chance to prove myself to You. When I feel unqualified or too young, too old, too uncertain for whatever You're calling me toward, remind me that You equip the people You call. Help me stop waiting to feel ready and start walking in obedience to what's right in front of me. I don't need to have it all figured out -- I just need to trust that You formed me on purpose. Give me courage like You gave Jeremiah. Amen.
