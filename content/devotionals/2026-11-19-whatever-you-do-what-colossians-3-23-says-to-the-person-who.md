## A Letter to People Who Didn't Choose Their Work

Paul wrote these words to a church in Colossae, but the verses right around it are addressed specifically to slaves — people with no say over their labor, their schedule, or their employer. That context matters. Paul wasn't writing to someone living their dream career. He was writing to people stuck doing work they never chose, under authority they didn't pick, with no path to quit and no HR department to appeal to.

And into that reality, he doesn't say "endure it" or "survive it." He says do it heartily, as to the Lord. The Greek word behind "heartily" is ek psyches — literally "from the soul." Not from obligation. Not from fear of a bad performance review. From the soul, the way you'd work if Christ himself were your direct supervisor standing beside your desk.

That reframes everything about work and career. Most of us don't struggle with slavery, but we do struggle with meaninglessness — the sense that our job is just a transaction, a thing we trade hours for money to do, disconnected from anything that actually matters. Colossians 3:23 doesn't deny that work can be hard, boring, or unfair. It relocates the audience. You're not ultimately performing for a manager, a client, or a paycheck. You're performing for the Lord, and he sees the spreadsheet nobody else reads and the extra effort nobody else notices.

## Why 'As Unto the Lord' Changes the Math

There's a strange freedom in this. If your work is ultimately for people, then unfair bosses, thankless tasks, and overlooked effort will eventually embitter you — because people are inconsistent judges. But if your work is for the Lord, the inconsistency of your workplace stops being the final word on whether your effort mattered. Paul goes on in verse 24 to say you'll receive an inheritance as a reward — language that would have landed hard on slaves who, under Roman law, could never actually inherit anything. He's telling the most overlooked workers in his world: your labor is seen, valued, and rewarded by someone whose opinion outlasts every performance review.

This doesn't mean career frustration is imaginary or that you should tolerate genuine injustice without pursuing change where you can. It means the discouragement of unseen effort doesn't have to own your heart. When your work and career feel invisible to everyone around you, this verse insists they are not invisible to God.

## Doing It Heartily Today

Practically, this reorients daily tasks that feel pointless. The email nobody reads carefully. The project that gets changed anyway. The job that isn't your calling but pays the bills while you wait for clarity about what is. "Heartily, as to the Lord" doesn't require loving every task — it requires offering it honestly, as worship rather than resentment.

Try this: before starting your workday, say quietly, "This is for you." Not as a magic phrase, but as a redirection of attention. It won't fix a toxic workplace or make a boring task thrilling. But it will remind you that your integrity and effort are not wasted on people who don't notice — they're witnessed by the One who does, and that is reason enough to do good work, even on the days career feels like nothing more than survival.

PRAYER: Lord, so much of my work feels unseen and unrewarded by the people around me. Help me remember that I'm ultimately working for you, not for approval or recognition from anyone else. Give me a heart that works honestly even when no one is watching or grateful. Turn my ordinary tasks into something offered to you rather than something merely endured. Thank you that you see the effort nobody else notices. Teach me to find purpose in this season of work, even while I wait for more clarity about my calling.
