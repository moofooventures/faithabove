## Spoken the Night Before the Cross

Jesus said these words during the Last Supper, hours before His arrest, in the middle of a long, intimate conversation recorded in John 13-17. He had just washed His disciples' feet and was explaining what it would mean to remain connected to Him like a branch to a vine. Love, in this context, isn't an abstract feeling -- it's the defining mark of what it means to follow Him. And then He said something His disciples wouldn't fully understand until the next day: the greatest expression of love is laying down your life for the people you love.

The word "friends" here is significant. In the ancient world, friendship between equals was considered a noble bond, but laying down your life was typically reserved for duty to a king, a nation, or a god -- not for ordinary people. Jesus flips that hierarchy. He says the highest form of love isn't owed to kings; it's given to friends, to the people sitting around that table with Him, including the one who would betray Him within hours. He wasn't speaking theoretically. Within a day, He would prove it.

## Love That Costs Something

It's easy to read "lay down his life" and think only of dramatic, once-in-a-lifetime sacrifice -- stepping in front of danger for someone else. That's real, but it's not the only application. The laying-down Jesus describes also happens in smaller, daily ways: setting aside your own comfort for your spouse, giving up your evening to sit with a grieving friend, forgiving someone who hasn't earned it, choosing patience with a child who's testing every bit of it. Love that costs nothing isn't the love Jesus is describing here.

This matters because our culture often defines love as a feeling that shows up and fades on its own schedule. John 15:13 defines love as a choice to put someone else's good ahead of your own, consistently, even when it's expensive. That's why Jesus connects it directly to friendship with Him in the following verses -- He calls His disciples friends specifically because of what He was about to do for them, not because of some casual closeness. Love, biblically, is proven in sacrifice, not just sentiment.

## Living This Out This Week

You probably won't be asked to die for anyone today, but you will be given smaller chances to lay something down -- your schedule, your pride, your need to be right, your comfort. Look for one relationship this week where love is asking you to give up something real: time, convenience, control, or the last word in an argument. That's where John 15:13 gets practical.

It also helps to remember you're on the receiving end of this verse before you're ever asked to live it out. Christ laid down His life for you first, while you were still His enemy, not His friend (Romans 5:8 says as much). That's the love that fuels your ability to love others sacrificially -- not gritted-teeth obligation, but a response to having been loved first. When loving someone feels like too much to give, go back to the cross and remember what was given for you. Then you'll find, slowly, that you have more to give than you thought.

PRAYER: Jesus, thank You for loving me enough to lay down Your life before I ever loved You back. Give me a love that looks like Yours, not just in words but in action. I can't love like this on my own -- fill me with Your love so it overflows. Amen.
