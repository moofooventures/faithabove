## Written to People Who Had No Career Choice at All

It's easy to slap Colossians 3:23 on a motivational poster and miss how radical it originally was. Paul wrote this letter around AD 60, and the verses right before it are addressed specifically to 'servants' -- doulos, the Greek word for slaves. In the Roman world, a huge portion of the workforce had zero say in their job, their hours, or their master. Work, for them, wasn't a calling; it was often forced survival under an owner who could be kind or cruel, fair or abusive. Into that reality, Paul doesn't tell them to revolt, nor does he tell them their work is meaningless because it isn't 'their choice.' He tells them something that must have sounded almost unbelievable: your labor has dignity because Christ sees it, regardless of who signs your paycheck.

The word 'heartily' translates the Greek ek psyches -- literally, 'from the soul.' Paul is saying: don't just go through the motions with your hands while your soul checks out. Work from the inside, not merely for the outside audience of a boss who may never notice or thank you. This reframes work entirely. It's not first about human approval, promotion, or fair treatment (though those matter). It's about doing the labor set before you as if Christ himself were the one receiving it.

## What This Means for Your Job Today

Most of us aren't enslaved, but many of us do work that feels unseen, underpaid, or unrewarding -- the meeting nobody remembers, the shift nobody thanks you for, the career that didn't turn out how you hoped. Colossians 3:23 doesn't erase the frustration of a hard job or a difficult boss, but it does relocate the audience. You're not ultimately performing for a manager's approval or a LinkedIn headline. You're offering your work, ordinary as it may be, to God himself. That means the accountant, the nurse, the barista, the stay-at-home parent, and the CEO all stand on identical ground: the size of the job matters less than the heart behind it.

This is deeply freeing for anyone stuck in a career season that feels stuck -- underemployed, overlooked, or simply grinding through Mondays. Your worth was never tied to your title. And your effort isn't wasted just because no human applauds it. The verse also cuts the other way: it confronts halfhearted, resentful work disguised as busyness. Working 'as to the Lord' means integrity when no one's watching, excellence when no one will know, and patience when the job is thankless.

## A Practical Way Forward

Tomorrow morning, before you walk into work -- whatever that looks like for you -- try naming it out loud: 'This is for you, Lord.' Let that reframe the inbox, the shift, the meeting, the diapers, the deadline. It won't fix a toxic workplace or unfair pay by itself, and Scripture elsewhere calls for justice in labor too. But it will steady your heart in the meantime, reminding you that your career, however ordinary, is seen by the God who assigns eternal weight to hidden faithfulness.

PRAYER: Lord, so much of my work feels unseen and unrewarded, and it's easy to grow resentful or numb. Remind me that you see every task I do, even the small and thankless ones. Help me work heartily, from my soul, as an offering to you rather than a performance for others. Give me integrity on the days no one is watching and patience on the days that feel wasted. Use my work, whatever it is, for your purposes. Amen.
