## Twelve Years of Being Unclean

Mark tells us this woman had suffered a continuous bleeding disorder for twelve years, had spent all she had on physicians, and had only gotten worse (Mark 5:25-26). Under Levitical law (Leviticus 15:25-27), her condition made her ceremonially unclean — not just physically sick, but socially and religiously isolated. Anyone she touched, or anything she sat on, became unclean too. For twelve years she likely couldn't embrace her family, enter the temple, or move through a crowd without risking exposure and scorn. Her healing wasn't just about ending pain; it was about re-entering a life she'd been locked out of.

This is the desperation behind her decision to push through a packed crowd toward Jesus. She didn't ask for a private audience or a formal prayer. She calculated — "If I may touch but his clothes, I shall be whole" — that the edge of his robe would be enough. In Jewish custom, the "hem" (or tzitzit, the tassels commanded in Numbers 15:38-39) carried symbolic weight, a visible reminder of covenant faithfulness. She wasn't grabbing at random fabric; she was reaching for the part of Jesus that represented God's promise-keeping nature, believing that even that much contact could reach her illness.

## Faith That Doesn't Wait for Permission

What's remarkable is that she expected to be found out and possibly rebuked for her boldness — an unclean woman touching a rabbi in public broke social convention sharply. Yet Jesus doesn't scold her. He stops, asks who touched him, and when she comes forward trembling, he says, "Daughter, thy faith hath made thee whole; go in peace, and be whole of thy plague" (Mark 5:34). He calls her "daughter" — the only time in the Gospels Jesus uses this term for anyone — restoring not just her body but her place in the family of God.

Healing in this story isn't a transaction of impressive faith performance; it's the quiet reach of someone who believed Jesus was accessible even in a crowd, even without an invitation, even carrying years of shame. If you're praying for healing right now — physical, emotional, or otherwise — this story reminds you that you don't need perfectly articulated theology or a front-row position. You need a willingness to reach toward Him with what little strength or hope you have left.

## What This Means for Your Healing Today

Many people wrestling with chronic illness or long unanswered prayers for healing find this account comforting precisely because it doesn't rush. Twelve years. Multiple doctors. All her money spent. God doesn't scold her for taking so long to arrive at faith; He meets her in the moment she finally reaches out. Healing, in Scripture, is rarely instant for the sufferer's journey, even when it's instant in its final moment.

If today you feel like you've tried everything and are running low on both resources and hope, let this story recalibrate what reaching for Jesus can look like. It doesn't have to be loud or eloquent. Sometimes it's simply deciding, in the middle of your crowd of circumstances, that even the edge of His presence is enough. Bring your condition honestly before Him. Ask for healing without pretending you have more faith than you do — she only had enough to touch a hem, and it was sufficient.
