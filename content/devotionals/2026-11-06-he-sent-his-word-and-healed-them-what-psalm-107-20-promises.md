## A Song Written for the Rescued

Psalm 107 is often called a psalm of thanksgiving for deliverance, and it reads like a collection of testimonies stitched together -- wanderers lost in the desert, prisoners in chains, sailors caught in a storm, and the sick, described here as being near death because of their own foolishness and sin. Each group cries out to the Lord in their trouble, and each time the refrain repeats: he delivered them. Verse 20 falls in the section about the sick and afflicted, and it gives us one of the most compact pictures of healing in the whole Old Testament: 'He sent his word, and healed them, and delivered them from their destructions.'

Notice the mechanism here. God doesn't simply decide to heal; he *sends his word*. In Hebrew thought, God's word was never just information -- it was active, creative power. In Genesis 1, God speaks and the world exists. Here, God speaks and bodies and lives that were headed toward destruction are turned around. This is healing understood not as a random act of mercy but as the direct, purposeful action of a God who speaks life into ruin.

## Healing That Reaches Further Than the Body

It's worth noticing that the healing in this psalm follows affliction brought on by foolish choices -- the text says plainly that some of the afflicted had rebelled against God's words and despised his counsel. And still, when they cried out, he healed them. This matters for anyone carrying shame along with sickness, wondering if their pain disqualifies them from God's help. The psalm says otherwise. The cry comes first, not the perfect record.

The phrase 'delivered them from their destructions' widens the lens too. This isn't only about physical healing -- it's about being rescued from a trajectory heading toward ruin, whatever shape that ruin takes: a body breaking down, a mind unraveling with worry, a life collapsing under consequences. Healing in Scripture is holistic. It touches the body, yes, but also the soul, the relationships, the story a person thought was already finished.

## What It Looks Like to Cry Out Today

If you're waiting on healing right now -- a diagnosis, a slow recovery, a chronic condition, a wound that won't close -- this psalm doesn't promise instant, visible results on your timeline. But it does promise this pattern: trouble, crying out, and God sending his word. That word still comes today through Scripture itself, through the quiet assurance of the Spirit, through the body of Christ showing up with casseroles and prayers and presence, through doctors and medicine God works through, and sometimes through a peace that settles in before the body even catches up.

The practical application is simple but not easy: cry out. Don't suffer in silence, assuming your pain is too small or your failures too large for God's attention. The sick in this psalm didn't heal themselves by trying harder; they called out, and the word was sent. Healing is still God's business, still his initiative, still something he delivers to those who ask. Whatever destruction you're facing today -- physical, emotional, relational -- bring your need honestly before the God who has never stopped sending his healing word to those who call on him.
