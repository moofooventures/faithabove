## A Broken City Hearing the Law Again

When Nehemiah spoke these words, Jerusalem's walls had just been rebuilt after decades of ruin. The people gathered in the square, and Ezra the priest read aloud from the Law of Moses — likely for the first time in a generation. As the people heard the words, many of them wept. They realized how far they'd drifted, how much had been lost, how much rebuilding still had to happen beyond just the stone walls around them.

It would have made sense for Nehemiah to let that grief sit. Instead, he tells them something surprising: stop mourning. Go feast. Share your food with those who have none. "The joy of the LORD is your strength." This wasn't joy because everything was fixed — the city was still recovering, the people were still vulnerable, the work was far from finished. The joy Nehemiah called for was rooted not in their circumstances but in belonging to God on a day set apart as holy.

## Joy Is Not the Same as Happiness

We often use joy and happiness interchangeably, but the Bible treats them differently. Happiness tends to rise and fall with what's happening around us. Joy, in Scripture, is sturdier — it's tied to who God is and what He has done, which doesn't change even when our circumstances do.

That's why Nehemiah could call for a feast in the middle of a rebuilding project, and why Paul could write about joy from a prison cell in Philippians, and why James could tell scattered, suffering believers to "count it all joy" when they faced trials. None of them were pretending things were fine. They were pointing to a deeper well — joy as strength, joy as fuel, joy as something you draw from rather than wait to stumble into.

## Choosing Joy in an Unfinished Season

Maybe your life right now feels like that half-rebuilt Jerusalem — some things restored, a lot still broken, and grief mixed in with gratitude. Nehemiah's words to that crowd apply to you too: don't let sorrow have the final word today. Not because your pain isn't real, but because joy in the Lord is available even now, and it's meant to strengthen you for whatever rebuilding still lies ahead.

Practically, that might mean choosing gratitude before you feel like it, sharing what you have with someone who has less (as Nehemiah told the people to do), or simply pausing to remember that today, whatever it holds, belongs to God. Joy isn't denial. It's defiance — a refusal to let unfinished circumstances have the last word over your heart. Let today be a day you draw strength from that joy, even in the middle of the rebuilding.
