## A Proverb for the Unseen Worker

Proverbs 22:29 is one of a cluster of wisdom sayings ancient Israelite scribes used to train young men entering public service, trade, or administration. It reads almost like a job-interview tip repackaged as a promise: "Seest thou a man diligent in his business? he shall stand before kings." The word for "diligent" (*mahir*) actually means skilled or swift — someone who has put in the time to become genuinely good at their craft, not just someone who shows up. "Business" here isn't limited to commerce; it covers any task or occupation a person is responsible for.

The promise attached to this diligence is striking: standing before kings. In the ancient Near East, access to a king wasn't casual. Royal courts were gatekept tightly, and only the most skilled scribes, craftsmen, or officials were ever summoned into a king's presence — think of Daniel, trained for three years before entering Nebuchadnezzar's service (Daniel 1:5), or Joseph, whose administrative skill eventually put him in front of Pharaoh himself (Genesis 41:46). The proverb isn't promising fame for its own sake; it's describing a natural outcome of skill cultivated faithfully over time: recognition finds its way to competence, even when competence doesn't go looking for it.

## Diligence When No One's Watching

Here's what makes this proverb land differently today: most diligent work happens in rooms no king ever enters. You do the report well even though your manager skims it. You show up early to a job that doesn't acknowledge it. You handle a difficult client with patience nobody above you will ever know about. The temptation in modern work and career is to assume that if recognition isn't immediate, the diligence doesn't matter — that skill without an audience is wasted effort.

Proverbs 22:29 pushes back on that. The proverb doesn't say "seest thou a man diligent in front of kings" — it says diligent "in his business," meaning in the actual day-to-day substance of the work, regardless of who's watching. The standing-before-kings part comes later, almost as a byproduct. This matches the broader wisdom tradition of Proverbs, which consistently ties long-term reward to patient, unglamorous consistency rather than attention-seeking performance (see also Proverbs 12:24, 13:4).

## Working Well in a Season That Feels Unseen

If your current job, business, or calling feels like it's going unnoticed — passed over for promotions, unseen by leadership, under-compensated for the skill it demands — this proverb offers both patience and purpose. Patience, because the verse doesn't promise instant recognition; it describes a pattern that plays out over time, the way Joseph's years in Potiphar's house and then prison preceded his years in Pharaoh's palace. Purpose, because it frames diligence itself as worth pursuing, independent of whether a king is watching this week.

A practical way to live this out: choose one area of your work and career where you've been tempted to coast because no one's checking, and do it with full skill anyway — not for the recognition, but because diligence is itself the proverb's subject, not the reward. Ask God for endurance to keep refining your craft in the unseen middle, trusting that the pattern Scripture describes still holds: skill cultivated faithfully rarely stays hidden forever, and even if it never reaches an earthly king, it's still being done, as Colossians 3:23 puts it, "as to the Lord, and not unto men."
