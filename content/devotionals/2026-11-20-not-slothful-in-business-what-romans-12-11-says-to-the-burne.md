## Three Short Phrases With a Lot Packed In

Romans 12 is Paul's turn from theology to practice — "I beseech you therefore... present your bodies a living sacrifice" (v.1) kicks off a chapter of rapid-fire instructions on how faith shows up in ordinary life. Verse 11 is only nine words in English, but it's built on three tightly connected ideas: don't be lazy in your work ("business" here is a broad word covering diligence and effort, not just commercial trade), stay spiritually fervent (the Greek *zeo* literally means to boil or be hot, like water at a rolling boil), and do it all as service to the Lord.

What's striking is the order. Paul doesn't say be fervent so that you'll work hard. He links diligence in work directly to fervency of spirit, and both to the ultimate audience of our labor: the Lord, not a boss, not a paycheck, not even personal ambition. For a church in Rome made up partly of enslaved people and free laborers working for masters who may never have noticed or rewarded their effort, this reframing was significant. Your work has worth regardless of who's watching, Paul says, because you're ultimately serving Christ through it.

## Burnout and the Missing Middle Word

Most people who are tired of their work fall into one of two patterns: busyness without fervor (going through the motions, clocking in and out emotionally even while physically present), or fervor without the discipline of actual diligence (passion that burns bright and fizzles, never producing steady fruit). Paul's verse holds both together. Diligence without spirit becomes drudgery. Spirit without diligence becomes burnout followed by crash. The two were meant to sustain each other.

If you're exhausted by your job right now, it's worth asking which piece is missing. Sometimes the fix isn't working harder, it's remembering why the work matters — reconnecting effort to purpose, to the people the work serves, to God who sees what no performance review captures. Other times the fix is the opposite: passion has outpaced sustainable practice, and what's needed is rest, structure, and realistic pacing, not more enthusiasm.

## Serving the Lord Through Monday Morning

Colossians 3:23 says almost the same thing in different words: "whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men." Paul clearly believed this truth needed saying more than once, probably because it's easy to forget on an ordinary Tuesday when the work is unglamorous — data entry, dishes, difficult coworkers, thankless shifts. The temptation is to assume spiritual fervor belongs in church and diligence belongs at work, as though they're unrelated categories. Paul merges them. Fervent spirit was never meant to be reserved for Sunday.

Practically, this might look like praying briefly before a shift starts, asking God to use the work itself as an act of worship. It might mean examining whether cynicism has crept into how you talk about your job, and choosing instead to do the next task with real effort, remembering who you're ultimately serving. It might mean setting boundaries so fervency doesn't curdle into burnout, because slothfulness isn't the only danger Paul is guarding against — joyless overwork is too.

Whatever your work looks like this week — exciting or exhausting, visible or invisible — Paul's short charge still applies. Don't coast. Don't burn out either. Stay diligent, stay spiritually alive to what you're doing, and remember whose eyes actually matter in the end.

PRAYER: Lord, I bring my work to You today — the parts I enjoy and the parts that drain me. Forgive me for the times I've gone through the motions or let cynicism replace effort. Reignite in me a fervent spirit, not just for Sunday but for Monday morning too. Help me remember that You see the work no one else notices, and that serving You is the real purpose behind it. Guard me from both laziness and burnout, and give me strength to be diligent in a healthy, sustainable way. In Jesus' name, amen.
