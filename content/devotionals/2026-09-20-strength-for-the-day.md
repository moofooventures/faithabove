Most mornings, strength feels like something you have to dig up on your own. You wake up tired, look at the list of what the day demands, and start rationing energy before you've even had coffee.

Isaiah's promise flips that around. The strength doesn't come from trying harder. It comes from waiting — from turning toward God before turning toward the to-do list. "Wait upon the LORD" isn't passive; it's the posture of someone who has stopped pretending they can do it alone.

**Three ways this shows up today:**

- **Mounting up like eagles** — the big moments where you need to rise above a hard situation.
- **Running and not growing weary** — the sustained effort of a long season: a hard job, a long illness, raising kids.
- **Walking and not fainting** — the slow, ordinary plod of daily life, which is often the hardest of the three.

Notice the order. Most of us want the eagle moment — the dramatic breakthrough. But Isaiah ends with walking, because that's where most of life actually happens. The same God who lifts you also holds you up when you're just putting one foot in front of the other.

PRAYER: Lord, I don't have the strength I need for today on my own. Thank You that I don't have to. Teach me to wait on You before I run ahead on my own energy. Renew me for whatever this day holds — the big moments and the small, ordinary ones. In Jesus' name, amen.
