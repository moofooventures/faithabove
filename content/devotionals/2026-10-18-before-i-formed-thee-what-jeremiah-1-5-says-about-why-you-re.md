## A Young Man Who Felt Unqualified

When God spoke this verse, Jeremiah was likely a teenager, maybe even younger, living in the small town of Anathoth just outside Jerusalem. His immediate response wasn't gratitude -- it was panic. 'Ah, Lord God! behold, I cannot speak: for I am a child,' he says in the very next verse. God had just told him he was ordained to be a prophet to entire nations, and Jeremiah's honest reaction was that he didn't have the resume for it.

That tension is worth sitting with, because most of us feel it too. We hear language about calling or purpose and quietly assume it applies to people more qualified, more confident, or further along than we are. But notice the order of events in this verse. God didn't wait to see what Jeremiah would become before assigning him purpose. He knew him, set him apart, and appointed him *before he was formed in the womb* -- before Jeremiah had done anything, said anything, or proven anything at all.

## Known Before You Could Perform

The Hebrew word translated 'knew' here (*yada*) is the same word used for deep, relational knowledge -- not just awareness, but intimate knowing. This wasn't God glancing at a future prophet from a distance. It was God knowing Jeremiah personally before Jeremiah had a pulse. The word for 'sanctified' means set apart for a specific purpose, and 'ordained' carries the sense of being appointed or established to a task.

This matters because so much of our anxiety about purpose or calling comes from thinking it's something we have to earn or discover through enough effort, enough success, or enough searching. Jeremiah's purpose wasn't the result of a long resume. It was settled before he existed. The call came first; the equipping came after, often through the very struggles and inadequacies he was afraid would disqualify him.

## What This Means for Your Today

If you're in a season of wondering what your life is actually for -- a career crossroads, a quiet sense that you're meant for more, or simply the ache of feeling unused -- this verse doesn't hand you a five-year plan, but it does something more foundational. It tells you that purpose isn't contingent on your current confidence level. God wasn't waiting for Jeremiah to feel ready. He was waiting for Jeremiah to say yes and keep showing up, often afraid, often unsure, all the way through decades of a genuinely hard ministry.

Your calling, like Jeremiah's, probably won't arrive as a dramatic voice from heaven. More often it looks like noticing what breaks your heart, what you keep being drawn back to, what doors keep opening even when you doubt yourself. The practical move isn't to wait until you feel equipped. It's to respond, like Jeremiah eventually did, by trusting that the One who formed you also formed a reason for you being here -- and that He'll supply what you lack as you go.

You don't need to have it all figured out today. You need only to believe that you were known and set apart before you ever had a chance to prove yourself, and let that truth quiet the voice telling you that you're too late, too unqualified, or too ordinary for whatever is next.
