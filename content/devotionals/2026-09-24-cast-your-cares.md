title: You Weren't Meant to Carry It Alone
date: 2026-09-24
topic: Anxiety
verse_ref: 1 Peter 5:7
verse_text: Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.
description: A short verse with a big instruction — and a reason that makes it possible.
---
Peter wrote this to Christians who were being persecuted — people with real, heavy, physical burdens, not abstract worries. And his instruction is a single verb: cast.

Casting isn't gently setting something down. It's an active, deliberate throw — getting rid of the weight entirely rather than holding onto part of it "just in case." Peter says to do this with "all" your care, not the manageable parts.

**Why this is possible, and not just nice advice:**

The verse doesn't stop at the instruction. It gives the reason: "for he careth for you." You're not casting your burden into a void and hoping. You're handing it to someone who is already paying attention, who already cares about the outcome more than you might think.

Most of us hold onto worry because letting go feels like carelessness — like if we stop worrying, we've stopped caring about the outcome. But Peter's logic runs the other direction: you can let go precisely because someone else is holding it with more capacity and more care than you have.

**Practically:** what's one specific care you're still holding today that you haven't actually handed over? Name it, and cast it — not once, but again, every time it creeps back.

PRAYER: Father, I keep picking back up what I've already handed to You. Today I'm naming this specifically and casting it on You, because You care about it more than my worry ever could. Thank You for carrying what I was never meant to carry alone. In Jesus' name, amen.
