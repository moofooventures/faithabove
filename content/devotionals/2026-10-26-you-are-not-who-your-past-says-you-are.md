## More Than a Fresh Start

Paul's words to the church at Corinth weren't written to people with tidy, respectable backgrounds. Corinth was a port city famous for its moral chaos, and many of the believers Paul addresses had come out of lives marked by idolatry, addiction, and relationships that would make modern readers blush. When Paul writes "if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature," he's not offering a self-improvement plan. He's describing a complete change of identity.

The Greek phrase translated "new creature" is *kaine ktisis* -- literally, a new creation. It's the same word used for the original act of creation in Genesis. Paul isn't saying you've been patched up or reformed. He's saying something has happened at the level of your very being, as radical as God speaking light into darkness. Your identity in Christ isn't an upgrade to the old you; it's a replacement.

## Old Things Passed Away

This matters because so much of our struggle with identity comes from believing our past still has the final word. Maybe it's a failure you can't forgive yourself for, a label someone else gave you, or a pattern of sin you assume defines you permanently. Paul says plainly: old things are passed away. Not improving. Not fading slowly. Passed away -- a done transaction.

The tense Paul uses in the Greek suggests a completed action with ongoing results. It already happened at the moment you came to Christ, and it keeps being true every day after. Your identity in Christ isn't something you have to re-earn each morning by having a good day. It was settled at the cross and resurrection, and it stands even on your worst days now.

This doesn't mean the process of change feels instant -- old habits, old thought patterns, old wounds often take time to work out practically. But the legal and spiritual reality underneath all of it has already shifted. You're not a sinner trying to become a saint. If you are in Christ, Scripture says you already are one, learning to live like it.

## Living Like the New Creation You Are

So how do you actually walk out an identity in Christ day to day? Start by noticing which voice you're listening to when you think about yourself. If it's naming you by your worst moment, your diagnosis, your divorce, your mistake, your family's dysfunction -- that voice is quoting from "old things," and Paul says those things passed away.

Instead, let your self-talk be shaped by what's actually true of you now: forgiven, adopted, loved, being made new. This isn't pretending your past didn't happen. It's refusing to let your past define your present. The behold in this verse is an invitation to look closely -- to actually examine the newness rather than assume you're still the same person you were.

Practically, this might mean refusing to introduce yourself (even silently, in your own head) by your old labels. It might mean forgiving yourself for something God already forgave you for years ago. Your identity in Christ isn't fragile -- it doesn't depend on your performance today. It rests on what He did, and that was finished long before you woke up this morning.

PRAYER: Father, thank You that my identity isn't built on my past mistakes or anyone else's opinion of me, but on what You've already done in Christ. Help me stop rehearsing old labels that no longer belong to me. Teach me to see myself the way You see me -- new, forgiven, Yours. When old shame tries to speak louder than Your truth, remind me that those things have already passed away. Make me more aware today of who I actually am in You. Amen.
