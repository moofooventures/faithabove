## Too Busy to Eat

Mark 6 drops us into a chaotic moment. The disciples have just returned from their first ministry trip, buzzing with stories of what they taught and healed. But before Jesus lets them debrief in peace, Mark notes the crowds pressing in so relentlessly that "they had no leisure so much as to eat." People were coming and going nonstop. It's a detail easy to skim past, but it's strikingly familiar—busyness so constant that even basic needs get crowded out.

Jesus' response isn't to hand out more assignments or praise their productivity. He says, "Come ye yourselves apart into a desert place, and rest a while." The Greek word for rest here, anapauo, means to refresh, to take a pause that restores strength—not simply to stop moving, but to be renewed. And notice where He sends them: a desert place, erēmos, an isolated spot away from demand and noise. Rest, in Jesus' model, often requires removing yourself from the very environment that's draining you.

## Rest Isn't a Reward for Finishing

One of the quiet assumptions many of us carry is that rest is something we earn after the to-do list is cleared. But the disciples hadn't finished their work—more people were still coming. The need didn't go away. Jesus pulled them aside anyway. This matters because it reframes rest not as a luxury for when things calm down, but as a necessity built into how we're meant to function, need or no need.

This is consistent with the rhythm woven into creation itself—God rested on the seventh day not because the world was finished needing care, but because rest was part of the design from the start. Jesus, fully aware that crowds would still be waiting when the disciples returned, still insisted on the pause. The ministry would survive a few hours without them. Their souls might not survive without it.

## What a Desert Place Looks Like for You

Few of us can literally escape to a desert, but the principle transfers directly. A desert place is anywhere you can step outside the noise long enough to be refreshed rather than simply busy. For one person, that's an actual day off with the phone away. For another, it's twenty quiet minutes with coffee before the house wakes up. For someone in a demanding season of caregiving or work, it might be a single boundary—one evening a week genuinely protected.

The honest obstacle is guilt. Like the disciples, we often feel that stepping away while people are still "coming and going" is irresponsible. But Jesus modeled this rhythm Himself, regularly withdrawing to pray and rest even during the busiest stretches of His ministry. If the Son of God needed to step apart and rest, we're not exempt from needing it either.

Rest isn't the absence of faithfulness—it's part of it. The crowds will still be there tomorrow. Your soul needs refreshing today.

PRAYER: Lord Jesus, I so easily believe rest is something I have to earn by finishing everything first. Help me find my own desert place, a real pause where I can be refreshed rather than just distracted. Forgive me for the guilt I carry when I stop moving. Teach me to trust that You can hold what I set down. Restore my strength the way only true rest can.
