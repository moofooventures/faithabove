## A Song Written for People Who Had Been Through It

Psalm 107 is a song of testimony -- it was likely sung communally, possibly after the Israelites returned from Babylonian exile, as they recounted all the ways God had rescued different groups of people: wanderers lost in the wilderness, prisoners in chains, sailors caught in storms, and here, the sick, who "drew near unto the gates of death" (v. 18). Each group cried out, and each time, the refrain repeats: "Then they cried unto the LORD in their trouble, and he saved them out of their distresses" (v. 19). This verse about healing isn't an isolated promise plucked out of nowhere -- it's one movement in a song specifically built to remind people what God has already proven He does.

"He sent his word, and healed them" ties healing directly to God's spoken word -- recalling Genesis, where God speaks and things come into being, and anticipating John 1, where the Word becomes flesh. Healing here isn't magic or mechanism; it's relational. God's own word, sent out, accomplishes what human effort and medicine of the ancient world could not.

## Healing That Includes More Than the Physical

The Hebrew word for "destructions" here (*shechituwth*) can refer to literal physical decay or affliction, but the whole psalm uses sickness as one picture alongside wandering, imprisonment, and storm -- all pictures of being trapped, helpless, and near the end of your own resources. That's worth noticing if you're praying for healing today, whether it's a body that won't cooperate, a mind under strain, or a situation that feels like it's eating you alive. This psalm doesn't separate physical healing from the larger pattern of God rescuing people who cry out from real trouble.

It's also worth noticing the order: they cried out first. The psalm doesn't promise healing as an automatic state of affairs -- it tells the story of desperate people bringing their desperation to God, and God responding. That's not a formula guaranteeing a particular outcome every time, but it is a consistent testimony: God hears the cry of those near death's gates, and He is not distant from suffering.

## What to Do While You're Waiting on Healing

If you're in the waiting right now -- test results pending, treatment not yet finished, a body that hasn't caught up to your prayers -- this verse offers two things. First, permission to cry out honestly, the way the psalm's subjects did, rather than performing strength you don't feel. Second, a reminder that God's healing has always come connected to His word -- which means Scripture itself isn't incidental to healing, but part of how God works on the soul even while the body is still recovering.

Practically, that might mean reading God's word deliberately during a season of sickness, not as a magic incantation but as a way of staying tethered to the God who has a long track record of sending healing into desperate situations. It might mean praying this psalm back to God: recounting what He's done before as a way of asking Him to do it again. Healing, in this psalm, is never separated from relationship -- it's the response of a God who hears, and sends His word, and delivers.

PRAYER: Lord, I bring You the places in me that need healing -- body, mind, and the parts of my life that feel like they're falling apart. Thank You that You are not distant from suffering, and that You hear when I cry out to You. Send Your word into my situation the way You have for others before me. Give me patience in the waiting and honesty in my prayers. I trust You as the God who delivers, even when healing takes time. Amen.
