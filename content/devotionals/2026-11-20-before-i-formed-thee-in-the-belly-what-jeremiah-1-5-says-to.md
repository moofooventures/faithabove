## A Young Man Who Felt Unqualified

Jeremiah's response to this word from God wasn't confidence — it was panic. "Ah, Lord GOD! behold, I cannot speak: for I am a child" (v.6). He was likely a teenager, from a small priestly town called Anathoth, being told he'd speak for God to entire nations. The gap between what God said about him and what he felt capable of must have felt enormous. That gap is worth sitting with, because most people who feel called to something — a vocation, a ministry, a season of change — feel it too.

The Hebrew word translated "knew" here is *yada*, a rich word implying intimate, personal knowledge, not mere awareness. God isn't saying He simply foresaw Jeremiah's existence; He's saying He knew him the way you know someone you love. And "sanctified" means set apart — before Jeremiah had done a single thing, good or bad, God had already marked out a purpose for his life. This wasn't earned. It was given.

## Calling Isn't Discovered, It's Remembered

One reason the search for purpose feels so exhausting for so many people is that it's often framed as a hunt — go find your calling, like it's hidden somewhere you haven't looked yet. Jeremiah's story suggests something different: purpose was already placed in him before he was born. His job wasn't to invent a calling from scratch; it was to step into one that preceded his own awareness of it.

This doesn't mean every believer gets a prophetic office. But the pattern holds: Psalm 139:13-16 says God formed us in the womb and ordained our days before one of them came to be. Ephesians 2:10 says we are God's workmanship, created for good works prepared beforehand. Purpose, biblically, is less about striving to become someone and more about uncovering who you were already made to be. That reframing matters on days when calling feels like static — when you don't know what you're supposed to do with your life, your career, or this current season. The question isn't "what will I make of myself," but "what has God already placed in me that I haven't yet walked into?"

## What This Means for Your Today

God's answer to Jeremiah's fear wasn't a pep talk about his hidden potential. It was a promise: "I am with thee to deliver thee" (v.8). Purpose and calling in Scripture are almost never paired with self-confidence; they're paired with God's presence doing the heavy lifting. That should relieve some pressure. You don't need to feel ready, articulate, or qualified today. Jeremiah didn't either.

If you're in a season where purpose feels foggy — between jobs, unsure of direction, wondering if your life matters — start smaller than "what's my grand calling." Ask instead: what has God clearly put in front of me right now, and am I being faithful there? Jeremiah's calling unfolded over decades, often in ways he didn't want (he became known as the weeping prophet, delivering hard words to a resistant nation). Calling rarely looks like instant clarity. It looks like showing up to the assignment in front of you while trusting that the One who formed you before birth isn't finished with you yet.

You were known before you were formed. That's not just Jeremiah's story — it's the pattern Scripture draws for every life shaped by God's hand.

PRAYER: Lord, thank You for knowing me before I was ever formed, for placing purpose in me before I had done anything to deserve it. I confess I often feel unqualified, unclear, or unsure of what I'm meant to do with my life. Help me stop searching for calling as though it were lost, and instead trust that You've already placed it in me. Give me courage like You gave Jeremiah, and remind me that Your presence matters more than my readiness. Use me today, right where I am. In Jesus' name, amen.
