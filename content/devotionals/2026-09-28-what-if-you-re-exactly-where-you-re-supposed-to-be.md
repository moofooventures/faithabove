## A Question Whispered at the Right Moment

Mordecai didn't send Esther a sermon. He sent her a question. The Jewish people were facing annihilation under a royal decree, and Esther, a queen who had kept her heritage secret, was the one person positioned to intervene. But approaching the king uninvited could mean death. Mordecai's words weren't a guarantee that everything would work out — they were a challenge to consider that her position, her timing, her whole strange and unlikely story, might not be an accident.

"Who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" is one of the most quietly powerful lines in Scripture about purpose and calling. Notice it's phrased as a question, not a promise. Mordecai doesn't claim to know God's plan with certainty. He simply invites Esther to consider that her circumstances — the ones she didn't choose, the ones that felt random or even unfair — might have been preparing her for exactly this moment.

## Your Calling Rarely Announces Itself

We tend to imagine purpose as something dramatic: a burning bush, a voice from heaven, a neon sign. But for Esther, purpose looked like years of ordinary life in a foreign palace before one moment of terrifying clarity. The Hebrew narrative of Esther is unusual because God's name never appears in the text at all — His presence is implied entirely through timing, coincidence, and the courage of people willing to act. That's often how calling works. It's less a lightning bolt and more a slow accumulation of circumstances that suddenly demand a decision.

If you're wrestling with what your purpose is, you may be looking for the wrong kind of sign. Ask instead: what has your life uniquely equipped you to do, see, or say that almost no one else in your position could? Your job, your family situation, your losses, your specific relationships — these aren't distractions from your calling. They may be the very training ground for it.

## Courage Is Part of the Calling

Esther's response to Mordecai is remarkable: "If I perish, I perish." She didn't wait until she felt fearless. She acted while still afraid, because she recognized that staying silent had its own risks. This is crucial for understanding purpose in our own lives. God doesn't usually call us into the safest option — He calls us into the necessary one. Discerning your calling isn't just a matter of figuring out what you're good at; it's also being willing to step into the room even when your hands are shaking.

Today, you may be facing a decision that feels too big, a door that seems terrifying to walk through, or simply the nagging sense that your life should add up to something more. Esther's story reminds us that purpose isn't found by escaping our current circumstances — it's found by leaning into them with courage, asking honestly whether this moment, this relationship, this job, this season, might be the very place we were made for.

## Living Today Like It Matters

You don't need to see the whole plan to take the next right step. Esther didn't know how the story would end when she agreed to approach the king. She only knew what was in front of her: a decision to make, a people to help, a moment that wouldn't wait. Your calling might not require anything as dramatic as hers. It might look like a hard conversation, a small act of generosity, or simply showing up faithfully where you already are. Whatever it is, consider today that you may have arrived here — right here — for such a time as this.
