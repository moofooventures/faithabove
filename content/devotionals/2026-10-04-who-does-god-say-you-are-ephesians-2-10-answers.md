## More Than a Mistake You Made

Paul writes Ephesians 2 to people who understood shame. The Ephesian church was full of former idol worshippers, people who had practiced magic, people whose old identities were wrapped up in a city famous for the temple of Artemis and its occult economy (Acts 19 even describes a public book-burning of magic scrolls once people came to faith). These were not people with clean records. And yet Paul doesn't lead with their past -- he leads with what God made them to be.

The Greek word translated "workmanship" is *poiema*, from which we get the English word "poem." It's the same word used elsewhere for something crafted, a work of art, intentional and purposeful -- not an accident, not a mistake, not a project still waiting to prove its worth. Paul is telling a room full of people with complicated pasts that their identity isn't rooted in what they used to do, or even what they currently struggle with. It's rooted in being God's handiwork, made for a purpose.

## Identity Before Assignment

Notice the order in the verse: we are His workmanship first, created for good works second. Identity precedes activity. This is the opposite of how most of us function day to day. We tend to build our sense of self from performance -- how productive we were, whether we said the right thing, whether we're keeping up with everyone else. But Paul insists that your identity in Christ isn't something you earn through good works; good works are the overflow of an identity God already gave you.

This is enormously freeing for anyone whose sense of worth has been tied to achievement, appearance, a job title, a relationship status, or old failures that still whisper accusations. If your identity is fundamentally "God's workmanship, created in Christ," then it isn't up for renegotiation every time you fall short. You're not what you did yesterday. You're not what someone said about you. You're what God made you -- and He doesn't make junk or accidents.

## Walking in What's Already True

The verse ends with "which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them" -- meaning the good works aren't random assignments you have to go find; they were planned in advance, specifically for you, before you even arrived to do them. That should quiet some of the anxious striving to find your purpose from scratch. You're not inventing your identity or your calling out of thin air. You're walking into something already prepared.

If you're struggling today with who you are -- comparing yourself to others, replaying old mistakes, wondering if you matter -- let this verse settle the question first. Before you do anything else today, you are God's handiwork. The good works will follow from that truth; they were never meant to establish it.

PRAYER: God, so much of how I see myself comes from what I've done, what I haven't done, or what others have said about me. Thank You for reminding me that my identity isn't something I have to earn or defend -- I am Your workmanship, made in Christ. Help me rest in that truth today instead of striving to prove my worth. Let the good works You've prepared for me flow out of who You say I already am. In Jesus' name, Amen.
