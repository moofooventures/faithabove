## Why Repeat Yourself Three Times?

Psalm 29:11 says essentially the same thing twice, with a third echo folded in: the LORD will give strength to His people. In Hebrew poetry, repetition isn't filler — it's emphasis, the ancient equivalent of underlining a sentence three times. David doesn't just want you to hear this promise; he wants it to be unmistakable, impossible to miss or explain away.

What's striking is where this verse lands. The first ten verses of Psalm 29 are a thunderstorm — "the voice of the LORD" breaking cedars, shaking the wilderness, striking like flames of fire. It's one of the most dramatic displays of raw power in the Psalms. And then, right after describing a God powerful enough to shake the earth, David pivots: that same thunderous power is the source of *your* strength, not just a force to be feared from a distance.

## Strength That Comes From Outside You

Notice the structure of the sentence: "The LORD will give strength." Not "the LORD will help you find your inner strength." Not "the LORD will remind you how strong you already are." The strength here is given, transferred, supplied — from Him to His people, because they don't generate it themselves.

This cuts against a lot of what we're told about strength today, which usually frames it as something you access through willpower, routines, or mindset. Scripture's picture is different: strength is a gift, repeatedly and deliberately given by God to people who are, by definition, not strong enough on their own. That's actually good news, because it means your strength for today isn't capped by how strong you felt yesterday or how resilient you naturally are. It's capped by nothing, because the source isn't you.

## Strength Paired With Peace

The verse doesn't stop at strength — it ends with blessing and peace. This pairing isn't accidental. In a storm-drenched psalm about God's overwhelming power, you'd expect the conclusion to be about fear or awe. Instead, David lands on peace. The same God powerful enough to break cedar trees and shake wilderness is the one who gives His people calm.

That combination matters for anyone trying to find strength in a hard season. Strength without peace becomes exhausting — gritting your teeth through difficulty with nothing to settle your spirit. But the strength God gives comes bundled with peace, which means it's not meant to leave you white-knuckled and depleted. It's meant to carry you through the storm *and* settle you once you're standing in it.

## Where to Find This Strength Today

If you're running low on strength right now — physically exhausted, emotionally worn thin, spiritually dry — this psalm doesn't tell you to dig deeper into yourself. It tells you to look toward the One whose voice can still shake wildernesses, and who has chosen, repeatedly and deliberately, to give that same power to His people.

Practically, that might mean pausing before you try to white-knuckle your way through today's hardest task and instead asking God plainly for strength — not as a last resort, but as the first move. It might mean remembering that the exhaustion you feel isn't evidence God has run out of strength to give; it's an invitation to ask for more of it. David said it three times so you wouldn't have to wonder: the LORD will give strength to His people. That includes you, today, in whatever is in front of you.

PRAYER: Lord, I come to You tired, aware that my own strength has limits that Yours doesn't have. Thank You for the promise that You give strength to Your people — not strength I have to manufacture myself, but strength that comes from You. I ask for that strength now, for the specific things in front of me today that feel too heavy. Along with strength, give me the peace this verse promises, so I'm not just enduring but resting even in difficulty. Be my power when I am faint. In Jesus' name, Amen.
