It's easy to forget that 1 Corinthians 13 wasn't written as a wedding reading. Paul wrote it to a church that was fractured by pride, competition, and division. He wasn't describing a wedding-day feeling — he was describing the daily, unglamorous discipline of loving people who annoy you.

Read the list again, slower, and swap "charity" for "love":

- **Patient** — with the person who's slow to change.
- **Kind** — even when kindness isn't returned.
- **Not envious** — of what someone else has that you don't.
- **Not proud, not self-seeking** — not making every interaction about being right.
- **Not easily angered** — not keeping a running tally of offenses.

None of that is a feeling. It's a set of choices, repeated daily, usually with someone specific in mind — a spouse, a coworker, a parent, a friend who's hard to love right now.

**Try this today:** pick one item from the list and one person. Practice just that one thing, on purpose, for this one day.

PRAYER: Lord, love like this isn't natural to me — my instinct is to keep score, not let things go. Help me practice patience and kindness with the specific person You're bringing to mind right now. Grow in me a love that isn't about being right, but about being like You. In Jesus' name, amen.
