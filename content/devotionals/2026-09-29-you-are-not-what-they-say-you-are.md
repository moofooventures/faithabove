## The Great Exchange

Paul writes this line near the end of a long argument about reconciliation, and it's easy to read past how staggering the claim actually is. He says God made Christ, who knew no sin, to 'be sin' for us—not just to bear the punishment for sin, but in some real sense to be identified with it—so that we could be made the righteousness of God in him. This is sometimes called the 'great exchange': Jesus takes what was ours (guilt, sin, shame) and gives us what was His (righteousness, standing, belonging).

This wasn't a small theological point for the Corinthian church. They were a congregation full of people carrying real reputational baggage—former idol worshippers, people entangled in sexual immorality, people the surrounding culture had already labeled. Paul isn't writing to spiritual elites; he's writing to people who needed a completely new answer to the question 'who am I?' because their old answer was written by their past.

## Your Identity Was Never Meant to Be Self-Made

So much of the pressure we feel today comes from trying to construct our identity out of performance, appearance, other people's approval, or our own worst moments. Culture tells us identity is something we build and defend. Scripture tells us something different: identity in Christ is something given, not earned—and it's sturdier than anything we could build ourselves, because it doesn't rest on our performance at all.

The word Paul uses for 'made' in 'made the righteousness of God' is a strong word—it's the same family of word used for something being fashioned into being, a decisive act rather than a gradual improvement project. You don't slowly earn your way into this identity through good behavior. It happens because of what Christ did, applied to you the moment you trust Him. That's a very different foundation than the shifting ground of self-esteem or public opinion.

## Living From Identity, Not Toward It

Here's where this gets practical. Most of us live as if our worth is still up for negotiation—dependent on this week's mistakes, this year's comparisons, or what a difficult person said about us. But if 2 Corinthians 5:21 is true, your identity in Christ is already settled. You're not working to become righteous enough for God to accept; you're already accepted, and you get to live out of that reality rather than toward it.

This changes how you handle a bad day, a harsh word, or an old memory that resurfaces with shame attached. Instead of asking 'does this prove something is wrong with me,' you can ask 'does this match what God has already declared true of me in Christ?' The answer is often no—and that's not denial, it's identity. Practically, try naming one lie about yourself you've been believing this week and holding it up against this verse. Let the exchange Christ made stand as the final word over whatever the world, your past, or your own inner critic keeps trying to say instead.

PRAYER: Father, thank You for the exchange You made through Christ—taking what was mine so I could receive what was His. I confess I often let shame or other people's opinions define who I am. Remind me today that my identity in Christ isn't something I earn or maintain, but something already given. When I feel unworthy, let this truth speak louder than my past. Help me live today out of who You say I am, not who I fear I might be. Amen.
