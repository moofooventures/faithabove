## A Pastor's Last Words to a Frightened Friend

Paul wrote 2 Timothy from a Roman prison cell, likely the Mamertine, a cold stone hole beneath the city where prisoners awaiting execution were lowered by rope. This wasn't a theoretical letter about fear. Paul was actually facing death, and he wrote to Timothy, a young pastor in Ephesus who by all accounts struggled with timidity, stomach ailments, and the pressure of leading a church under threat of persecution.

The Greek word translated "fear" here is *deilia*, and it's worth noting this is different from the healthy fear of God mentioned elsewhere in Scripture. *Deilia* means cowardice or timidity, a shrinking-back kind of fear that paralyzes. It's the only time this exact word appears in the New Testament, which tells us Paul chose it deliberately. He wasn't addressing reverence for God. He was addressing the gut-level fear that makes you want to hide, quit, or stay silent when you should speak.

## Three Things Fear Isn't From

Paul's structure here matters. He doesn't just say "don't be afraid." He tells Timothy what spirit he actually received instead: power, love, and a sound mind. In the original language, these three stand opposite the paralysis of fear point by point.

Power (*dynamis*) is the same word used for the resurrection power that raised Christ from the grave. Love (*agape*) is the selfless, others-focused love that casts out self-protective fear, echoing what John later wrote in 1 John. And a sound mind (*sophronismos*) refers to self-discipline, clear thinking, a mind not scattered or overwhelmed by anxious thoughts.

This matters because fear often convinces us we're powerless, unloved, and losing our grip on reality. Paul's answer isn't a pep talk. It's a reminder of identity. If you belong to God, this isn't the spirit you were given. Fear may visit, but it doesn't get to move in and unpack its bags.

## When Fear Feels Bigger Than the Verse

Maybe you're facing a diagnosis, a layoff, a phone call you're dreading, or a decision that could change everything. Bible verses for fear like this one aren't magic words that erase the circumstance. Timothy still had real threats around him. Paul still died in that prison not long after writing this letter. The promise isn't that fear-inducing situations disappear.

The promise is about what spirit governs you inside those situations. You can be afraid and still walk in power. You can feel the tremble and still choose love over self-preservation. You can have shaking hands and still think clearly enough to take the next right step.

Practically, this looks like naming your fear honestly instead of stuffing it down, then asking which of the three gifts you need to lean into today. Do you need to remember God's power is available to you, not just his? Do you need to choose love toward someone instead of retreating into isolation? Do you need to get quiet enough for a sound mind to actually function instead of spinning in worst-case scenarios?

Timothy likely read this letter and then had to walk out his front door into a city that didn't want him teaching about Jesus. You'll likely close this devotional and walk into whatever is making your stomach tighten today. The spirit of fear was never handed to either of you. Something better was.

PRAYER: Father, I confess the fear that's been gripping me lately, the kind that makes me want to shrink back instead of stand up. Thank you for reminding me that this isn't the spirit you gave me. Fill me instead with your power when I feel weak, your love when I want to protect myself instead of others, and a sound mind when my thoughts spiral. Help me take one honest step forward today, even if my hands are still shaking. I trust that you are with me in the exact situation I'm afraid of. In Jesus' name, amen.
