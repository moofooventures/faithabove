title: A Plan for a Future You Can't See Yet
date: 2026-09-22
topic: Hope
verse_ref: Jeremiah 29:11
verse_text: For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end.
description: This verse was written to people in exile. It wasn't a promise of comfort now — it was a promise of purpose later.
---
This is one of the most quoted verses in Scripture, and also one of the most misunderstood out of context. God said this to Israelites who had just been dragged into 70 years of exile in Babylon. It wasn't a promise that things would be easy. It was a promise that the story wasn't over.

The verse right before it tells them to build houses, plant gardens, and settle in — in other words, to keep living faithfully in a season that wasn't the one they wanted. The plan wasn't an escape from exile. It was a future on the other side of it.

**If you're in a season that doesn't feel like "the plan":**

- The waiting isn't wasted time. Something is still being built even when you can't see it.
- "Thoughts of peace, and not of evil" doesn't mean the absence of hardship — it means God's intent toward you isn't harm, even when the season is hard.
- An "expected end" is a hoped-for outcome, not necessarily a fast one.

You may be in your own version of exile right now — a job you didn't choose, a diagnosis, a delay you didn't plan for. This verse doesn't promise it ends tomorrow. It promises it isn't the whole story.

PRAYER: Lord, I don't always see the plan, especially in seasons like this one. Help me to keep building and planting even when I'm still waiting. Thank You that Your intentions toward me are for good, even when the path doesn't look like what I expected. In Jesus' name, amen.
