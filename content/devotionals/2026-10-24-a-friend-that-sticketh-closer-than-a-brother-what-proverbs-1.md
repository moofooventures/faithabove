## A Proverb With a Hidden Warning

Most English translations, including the KJV, render the first half of Proverbs 18:24 as a positive instruction: to have friends, be friendly. But many Hebrew scholars note that the underlying text is more ambiguous and possibly even critical — some translate it closer to "a man of many companions may come to ruin," contrasting the dangers of wide, shallow social circles with the value of one deeply loyal friend. Either reading points to the same core wisdom: quantity of relationships is not the same as quality, and genuine community requires something sturdier than mere acquaintance.

The second half is unambiguous and striking: "there is a friend that sticketh closer than a brother." In the ancient Israelite world, family loyalty — particularly brotherly bond — was assumed to be the strongest human tie available, rooted in covenant, inheritance, and tribal identity. For the author to say a friend could stick closer than that was a radical claim about chosen relationship rivaling, even surpassing, blood relationship. This isn't a throwaway compliment about having a nice friend group. It's pointing to the kind of friendship that functions like family — loyal under pressure, present in crisis, unwilling to leave when things get difficult.

## Why Friendship Takes Initiative

The structure of the verse also matters: before you receive a sticking-closer friend, you're told to "shew himself friendly." The Hebrew verb carries a sense of active loyalty — not passive likability, but intentional faithfulness extended toward others. Community doesn't happen to you; it's something you participate in building. This cuts against a modern tendency to wait for friendship to arrive fully formed, as though the right people will simply show up without any initiative on our part.

Scripture elsewhere backs this up with real examples. David and Jonathan's friendship in 1 Samuel 18 is described as Jonathan's soul being "knit" to David's, and it cost Jonathan politically and personally to stay loyal to his friend over his own father's wishes. Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 reminds us that two are better than one because when one falls, the other can lift them up. The biblical vision of friendship and community isn't casual; it's covenantal, sacrificial, and mutual.

## Building the Kind of Friendship That Sticks

If you're lonely or find yourself surrounded by many acquaintances but few real friends, Proverbs 18:24 offers both comfort and direction. The comfort: you don't need a hundred friends. One or two who stick closer than family is the biblical standard of rich community, not an exception to it. The direction: that kind of friendship rarely falls into your lap. It's built through showing up consistently, being trustworthy with someone's struggles, and choosing to stay present even when it costs you time, convenience, or comfort.

This week, consider who in your life might need you to "shew yourself friendly" first — a text checking in, an honest conversation, an offer to help carry something heavy. And consider, too, who has already stuck closer than a brother or sister to you, and whether they know how much that loyalty has meant. Real community is forged over time, through both ease and difficulty, and Scripture treats it as one of God's clearest gifts for getting through a hard world — not alone, but alongside people who stay.
