## A Word for the Exiles

Isaiah wrote these words to a people in Babylonian exile, far from home, wondering if God had forgotten them. They weren't tired from a hard week at work -- they were tired from decades of displacement, watching their city burn, raising children in a foreign land under foreign gods. When Isaiah says "they that wait upon the LORD," the Hebrew word is *qavah*, and it doesn't mean sitting around doing nothing. It carries the idea of a rope being stretched taut, or thread being twisted together under tension. Waiting on God, biblically, is an active, strained, hopeful posture -- not passive resignation.

That distinction matters because so much of our search for strength today is really a search for a shortcut. We want the eagle's wings without the waiting. But Isaiah's order is deliberate: wait, then renew, then mount up, then run, then walk. Strength in Scripture is rarely instant. It's relational. It comes from staying close to the source.

## Three Kinds of Strength, One Source

Notice the progression in the verse: soaring, running, walking. Commentators have long pointed out this isn't random -- it's actually a descending order of difficulty, and that's the surprising part. Soaring on wings sounds hardest, but anyone who has lived through a long illness, a slow grief, or years of caregiving knows that the mountaintop moments of supernatural strength are often easier to access than the daily grind of simply walking without fainting. Isaiah promises God's strength for all three: the dramatic rescue, the steady sprint, and the exhausting plod of an ordinary Tuesday.

This is good news if your need for strength isn't a crisis but a slow leak. Maybe it's a marriage that requires patience you don't have, a body that aches more than it used to, a job that drains you before 9 a.m. God's promise of strength isn't reserved for heroic moments. It covers the walking, too.

## What Waiting Looks Like on a Tuesday

Practically, waiting on the Lord looks like showing up -- opening Scripture even when you feel nothing, praying even when the words feel hollow, staying in community even when you'd rather isolate. It's the twisting of the rope, strand by strand, until it can hold weight it couldn't hold before. Strength doesn't bypass weakness; it's built through staying tethered to God in the middle of it.

If you're running on empty today, you don't need more willpower. You need to exhale the striving and lean into the waiting -- the kind that trusts God is still working even when you can't feel wings under you yet.

PRAYER: Lord, I come to You worn thin, with less strength than I need for today. Teach me what it means to wait on You, not as passive giving up, but as active trust. Renew what's been depleted in me -- my patience, my hope, my energy for the tasks in front of me. When I can't soar, let me at least walk without fainting, holding onto You the whole way. Thank You that Your strength isn't limited to my best days. In Jesus' name, Amen.
